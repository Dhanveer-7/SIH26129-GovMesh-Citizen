Imagine a citizen moving to a new locality who needs to update their official address. Today, that single change requires updating property records with the Revenue Department, ration card details with Food and Civil Supplies, and residency status with Rural Development. That means visiting three separate offices, filling out three forms, and following three disconnected systems.

The problem is not a lack of digitalization. Each department already has its own operational database and officer workflows. The real issue is interoperability — these systems exist in silos and cannot talk to each other.

We are Team MESH, and what you see on the screen right now on our Citizen Portal is our live working prototype: GovMesh. 

GovMesh is an interoperability layer that connects independent departmental systems, allowing a citizen to submit a single request while each department processes it independently through its own authorized officers.

Thank you, Dhinith.

Behind the live demonstration you just watched, we engineered three key safeguards:

First, Traceability: Notice the Correlation ID displayed on Tab 1 and matching across all three officer dashboards. This common identifier allows end-to-end tracking across separate departmental audit logs.

Second, Data Integrity: The prototype generates SHA-256 cryptographic hashes of the citizen payload and supporting evidence, visible right here. This guarantees that no document or field can be tampered with between systems.

Third, Reliability and Idempotency: Our ingress APIs prevent duplicate records. If a citizen double-clicks Submit or experiences a network retry, GovMesh recognizes the transaction and avoids duplicate cases. Furthermore, failure isolation ensures that if one department server is delayed, the others continue processing normally.
